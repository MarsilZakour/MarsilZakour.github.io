About

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the Technical University of Munich working on 3D computer vision and machine learning. My research focuses on 4D hand–object interaction: reconstructing hands and objects in 3D and over time from video, modelling contact, and building the multi-sensor capture systems, simulators and datasets behind them. I am broadly interested in real-time perception, differentiable optimization, and embodied AI.

I have a background in software engineering, which shapes how I work: I design and build complete systems, from sensor drivers, synchronization and calibration to real-time perception pipelines and live demos. I also have a competitive-programming background, as a contestant, coach and judge.

Systems & sensors

I design and build complex multi-sensor systems, from hardware and drivers through synchronization, calibration and recording to live perception, behind the datasets and methods above. I work with both real sensors and synthetic data.

  • Middleware and infrastructure — ROS 1 and ROS 2 for multi-sensor, multi-machine capture, time synchronization, recording and replay, and real-time pipelines connecting sensors, perception and visualization.

Real sensing

  • Multi-camera rigs — synchronized multi-view RGB-D capture with RealSense cameras (RGB plus IR stereo at 30 Hz), multi-machine ROS recording, ChArUco-based calibration at about 0.8 px reprojection error, stereo depth and multi-view hand/object annotation on top.
  • Single-camera and egocentric setups — real-time hand tracking from one head-mounted or tabletop RealSense D435i camera (RGB only as model input; the IMU accelerometer levels the world frame, and depth serves only for visualisation), plus egocentric benchmarks (HOT3D, ARCTIC) for online world-space hand mesh recovery.
  • Kinect and Azure Kinect — depth sensing for body and hand capture, including time-of-flight depth from the Azure Kinect.
  • Leap Motion controller — ~110 Hz hand skeleton, evaluated as a low-latency anchor for interactive VR projects, and a CPU-only webcam tracker for comparison.
  • OptiTrack motion capture — marker-based optical tracking for ground-truth 6-DoF object poses, combined with the multi-view RGB-D cameras for dataset annotation.

Synthetic data

  • Unity — the engine behind my synthetic-data work: simulated 3D scenes with animated humans and objects, and interactive real-time (VR) applications.
  • HOIsim — Unity-based simulator for realistic synthetic 3D human–object interaction data, for training and sim-to-real studies.
  • Unity Perception — automatic ground-truth labelling (2D/3D boxes, segmentation, keypoints) and domain randomization of scenes, lighting and objects for synthetic datasets.
  • Rendered digital twins — Gaussian-based reconstructions of real captures that can be re-rendered from new views and driven by new hand motion.
